## Source and baseline assessment

The existing plugin selects shared `MultibandLr4Crossover`/`MultibandLr8Crossover`, advances smoothing per sample, and retains a coefficient-update countdown across callbacks. LR8 uses four biquads per low/high path with the two stated Butterworth Q values repeated; its reset reconstructs its vectors. Thus the proposed LR48 reference is consistent with the actual filter family, and the reset allocation concern is source-grounded.

For fixed cutoffs, the proposed branch formula telescopes to the product of `L_j + H_j`; these LR4/LR8 sums have unity magnitude. Each earlier band's later-stage compensation needs independent persistent state. The final high band remains unchanged; earlier bands intentionally change phase. This identity does not establish time-varying reconstruction under automation.

The public reference independently evaluates bilinear-prewarped complex polynomials. I inspected the terminal public and actual DawHost capture logs: ten public legacy cases and both host slopes pass their legacy-reference checks. Their non-unity sums reproduce the documented cascade limitation. These passing captures are not passing compensation tests. The report now correctly qualifies the missing original run-bound manifests and initialized-only reset result. The revised after-audio reset tests still require execution.

The preserved archive hashes and root's full-vector integrity/host comparison receipt are recorded in `audit/band-split-phase-compensation-gap.md`. Current captures are 48 kHz stereo coherent-tone baselines, not general numerical or lifecycle evidence.
