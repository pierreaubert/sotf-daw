## Mathematics accepted

The Butterworth pole formula gives the left-half-plane unit-circle poles. Normalize the product numerator to unit DC; the highpass substitution into that normalized transfer gives positive unity high-frequency gain. Canonical BW polarity is appropriate: do not invert selected outputs to force a sum target. Endpoints should be evaluated analytically in the oracle to avoid dividing by zero.

LR12 is squared first-order Butterworth with negative emitted HP. Its signed sum is `(1-u)/(1+u)`, a unity-magnitude allpass on the imaginary axis. LR24 and LR48 retain positive HP. Applying the signed HP consistently in the serial prefix and compensation factors makes the stated multiway telescoping identity correct. At very high frequency the last LR12 multiway band can have sign `(-1)^number_of_splits`; that follows the accepted formula and must appear in tests/help rather than being corrected afterward. Existing LR24 realizations and automation behavior remain on their preserved path.

For Bessel, setting `a² = 3*(sqrt(5)-1)/2` solves `a⁴+3a²=9`, so `3/((a*u)^2+3*a*u+3)` has magnitude 1/sqrt(2) at u=j. Write the normalized function explicitly as `L_norm(u)` and `H(u)=L_norm(1/u)` to avoid confusing it with the unnormalized polynomial named L(x). Both endpoint gains are positive. The [SciPy documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.bessel.html) supports distinguishing magnitude normalization from phase/delay normalization and cautions about digital delay flatness after the bilinear transform. No vendor-coefficient identity or digital linear-phase claim follows.

An independent Python pole-product probe (not SOTF code evidence) checked BW orders 1–8 at cutoff, Bessel scale 1.3616541287161306 and both cutoff magnitudes 0.7071067811865476. Across 801 logarithmic analog-frequency samples, LR12/24/48 signed-sum magnitude errors were <=2.45e-15. Non-LR serial splitting correctly has no unity-sum promise; compare its actual branch products and sum.
